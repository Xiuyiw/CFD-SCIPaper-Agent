# Literature-supported Introduction and Discussion

Use with the current paper spine, section duties, supplied literature records and readable sources.
This is host writing guidance, not an autonomous search service or a new approval workflow.
Reuse shared literature identities across sections; do not create separate identities merely
because the same work has a different role in Introduction and Discussion.

## Establish what a source supports

- Read the shared identity and the supplied excerpt at its page, section, paragraph or other locator.
  Check enough surrounding text to establish the object, conditions, method and qualification.
- A title, DOI, bibliographic match or existing locator identifies a source; none proves that a
  proposed claim is supported. Never invent a finding from metadata or an unread full text.
- Relate each proposed use to its claim, literature role, excerpt/locator and recorded support
  status. A role can be background, method basis, mechanism evidence or a contextual comparison.
- `supported` is a claim-specific assessment, not blanket authority for every use of the paper.
  Confirm that the actual passage supports the proposed wording and degree of certainty.
- `unsupported` means do not cite the record as support for that claim. Narrow the claim to what
  the passage establishes, use another supplied source, or omit it; do not silently relabel it.
- `needs-review` remains unresolved. Locate the needed passage or ask a precise question if it
  changes the argument; otherwise continue independent sections and keep this use out of asserted prose.
- When text conflicts with a recorded status, report the specific mismatch in the existing notes.
  Neither a status label nor a successful assembly check replaces semantic reading.
- Preserve excerpt attribution. Use short quotations only when necessary; normally paraphrase the
  supported meaning without copying source prose or removing important qualifications.

## Build an Introduction around the problem

Begin with the scientific or engineering need that makes this question consequential, then identify
the physical constraint or unresolved relation. Do not begin with an inventory of papers or software.
Use the supplied literature to explain what is established and what remains uncertain in that relation.
Where evidence warrants it, contrast competing explanations or methods and state what observation
could distinguish them. Do not manufacture a controversy to make the study seem novel.

Make the gap precise: which coupling, operating range, scale, metric or comparison is unresolved,
and why does it matter? Absence from the supplied reading set is not proof that no prior study exists.
Avoid absolute novelty claims such as "the first" without an adequately supported literature basis.
An honest bounded gap is stronger than a sweeping assertion that previous studies ignored the problem.

End by connecting this study's actual comparisons and evidence to that gap. Describe the question,
approach and bounded contribution, not promised results or a long list of routine workflow steps.
Keep simultaneous changes in geometry, operating input or physical pathways visible when they
prevent a single-factor causal interpretation. Scope the paper to what its evidence can answer.
These are argument duties, not a mandatory paragraph count or fixed sequence of stock sentences.

## Integrate literature in Discussion

Start from the current Results and ask what the combined evidence explains. Select useful numerical
anchors and spatial observations rather than repeating every table entry or caption.
Use literature at the point where it clarifies a mechanism, tests an interpretation, explains a
difference, or establishes the conditions under which a comparison is meaningful.

Compare compatible quantities and scopes: local versus integral, normalized versus absolute,
steady versus transient, model versus experiment, and the relevant boundary conditions.
Agreement may support an interpretation within those limits; it does not validate this simulation.
A mechanism reported elsewhere is a candidate here until the present evidence supports its transfer.
Explain material differences rather than deleting conflicting diagnostics or treating different
definitions as contradictions. An algebraic decomposition alone is not new causal evidence.

When several results support one explanation, state the distinct role of each. When plausible
alternatives remain, identify the missing discriminating evidence without implying it was acquired.
Separate a local diagnostic that locates changes from a controlled comparison that tests causality.
Put necessary limitations beside the affected claim or in the appropriate Methods/Discussion scope;
do not append repeated defensive sentences to every paragraph or hide consequential assumptions.

## Preserve identities and finish the section

Use the citation syntax supported by the current task package, retaining shared source identities
and the claim-specific evidence links. Do not hard-code display numbers from an older draft.
Check that the final cited sentence still matches the excerpt, role and support status after editing.
Keep unresolved source requests in evidence notes rather than turning them into manuscript text.
Protect author-edited citation fields; visible bibliography counts alone do not prove identity survival.
If a source or scientific definition changes, re-read the affected claims and neighboring argument.
Numeric replacement or reference renumbering does not automatically update their scientific meaning.
